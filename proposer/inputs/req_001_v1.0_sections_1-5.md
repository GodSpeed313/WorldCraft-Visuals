## 1. Your task, inputs, and sources

**Your task.** You are given two characters. Determine whether a defensible fusion of them exists. If it does, propose one. If it does not, say so and show your reasoning. A careful report that no defensible fusion exists is a valid and valued outcome. A fabricated connection is not.

**What you receive.** You receive exactly two authorized character profiles as case-specific source material, together with this requirements document, the pair-file schema, and the permitted provenance and validation definitions. You receive no authored fusion solutions, private evaluation keys, reviewer findings, or reserved-pair information.

**Source discipline**

- Treat the two profiles as your only evidence about the characters. Do not draw on outside knowledge of characters, stories, or genres to fill gaps.
- Every claim about what a property does must be supported by what that property states, or by a reasonable inference you identify as an inference.
- Never attribute to a property a capability, restriction, condition, or fact it does not state or reasonably support.
- Where the profiles are silent or ambiguous on something your fusion depends on, say so explicitly rather than resolving it silently in your favor.

## 2. How to construct a fusion

Work through these steps for each candidate connection you consider.

1. **Identify meaningful contributions from both parents.** Find at least one property from each character that the fusion actually depends on. A property that only adds appearance, mood, or theme is not a meaningful contribution.
2. **Construct a causal relationship that changes how an ability operates.** State how the two contributions act on each other. A fusion is not two abilities placed side by side, used one after the other, or active at the same time without affecting each other.
3. **Distinguish inherited capabilities from new effects.** Decide honestly whether the result is an inherited ability working differently, or a capability neither parent has. Both can be acceptable; misdescribing one as the other is not.
4. **Preserve hard limitations and prohibitions.** A fusion may not contradict any property either parent marks as a hard limitation or prohibition.
5. **Derive limits from the interaction.** Identify what the fusion cannot do because of how the two contributions combine, not only the limits each parent already had.
6. **Acknowledge uncertainty, and decline unsupported combinations.** If a connection depends on something the profiles do not establish, disclose it. If no connection survives the tests below, return a no-fusion report.

**Account for activation conditions.** Where properties have activation conditions, a fusion that depends on them can operate only when those conditions can hold at the same time. Show that they can, or treat the conflict as an unresolved question.

### The four defensibility tests

A candidate connection is defensible only if it passes all four.

1. **Both parents:** the connection depends on at least one cited property from each parent.
2. **Does work:** removing the connection meaningfully changes how at least one inherited ability operates. Identify the affected ability, explain the causal mechanism, and declare the expected shared structure and emergence levels. Your declarations are claims, not validated scores.
3. **Grounded limit:** at least one meaningful limitation follows from the interaction itself, rather than solely from an inherited parent limitation. Identify its causal basis.
4. **No violations:** the connection must not contradict either parent's hard limitations or prohibitions. Disclose any unresolved potential contradiction; it cannot be treated as passing. Either resolve it from the available source properties or reject the candidate as insufficiently supported.

These tests set a minimum. Passing them makes a proposal eligible for review; it does not make the proposal accepted.

**Disclosure is not a pass.** Disclosing an uncertainty does not establish that a candidate passes the defensibility tests. If an unresolved assumption is necessary for the connection to work, or to avoid a hard-constraint violation, the candidate cannot be submitted as defensible. An honestly declared Invented Bridge is still subject to every test.

## 3. Levels and provenance you declare

**Emergence level.** Declare 2 or 3. A connection that only reaches level 1 is not a fusion; report no-fusion instead.

- **Emergence 2:** the interaction meaningfully changes how an inherited ability is activated, targeted, controlled, or applied, without creating a capability beyond those of the parents. If your fusion only changes when, whether, or how an inherited ability is used, declare 2.
- **Emergence 3:** the interaction enables a capability or effect neither parent could achieve independently. Explain why neither parent could achieve it alone.

**Shared structure level.** A connection is at level 3 when a causal connection between properties from both parents explains why at least one ability works as proposed. A connection that names both parents but does not change how any ability works is below the required level.

Declaring 2 when the result is 2 is better than claiming 3. Your declaration is compared with independent review; accuracy counts, inflation does not help.

**Interaction provenance.** For each proposed fusion interaction, declare either Defensibly Derived or Invented Bridge, using the authoritative definitions in the WorldCraft Fusion Engine Requirements & Architecture Specification v0.1, §9.

- **Defensibly Derived:** every necessary inferential step follows from accepted source properties without introducing a new causal mechanism, permission, exception, conversion, domain authority, delivery mechanism, interaction pathway, or governing principle.
- **Invented Bridge:** the interaction requires a new causal rule not already established or defensibly entailed by the source properties. Identify and disclose that rule, its operational boundaries, and its effects on inherited limitations.

Source-Derived remains a valid provenance classification for directly established properties, but is not a permitted declaration for a newly constructed fusion interaction in this output format.

Unresolved remains an authoritative provenance state. If you are unsure whether an interaction requires a new causal rule, classify it as Unresolved, not as Invented Bridge. If an essential interaction cannot be classified defensibly, do not present it as a valid fusion; record the uncertainty and the rejection reason as a rejected candidate in a no-fusion report.

Any resulting ability inherits the weakest provenance among its required dependencies and governing interaction rules. Invented ancestry must remain visible throughout downstream derivations. Correctly declaring an Invented Bridge does not, by itself, establish that the proposed fusion is defensible or acceptable.

## 4. What you must produce

Return exactly one of two outputs.

**A. A fusion proposal,** containing:

1. **A draft pair file that passes the pair-file schema:** interaction, emergent ability, limitations, trait outcomes covering every property of both characters, exclusions, and the visual block.
2. **A causal explanation** stating what each parent's property contributes (by property id), how those contributions interact, and whether the resulting capability exceeds what either parent could accomplish independently. For Emergence 2, identify the meaningful modification without claiming a new capability; for Emergence 3, explain why neither parent could achieve the result alone.
3. **Declared levels:** shared structure and emergence.
4. **A declared provenance tier** for the interaction.
5. **A cited source for every limitation:** the property or mechanism it follows from.
6. **Disclosed uncertainties:** anything the fusion depends on that the profiles do not establish, and any unresolved potential contradiction.

**B. A no-fusion report,** containing:

1. A statement that no defensible fusion was found.
2. Up to three meaningfully distinct candidate connections you examined, each with the specific test or tests it failed and the property ids involved. Reworded versions of the same mechanism do not count as separate candidates.
3. If fewer than three distinct candidates could reasonably be identified, an explanation of why.

## 5. How your output is judged

- **Mechanical checks come first.** Checks apply according to output type. A fusion proposal is checked against the pair-file schema and the proposal requirements: cited property ids exist on the named parents, the interaction depends on at least one property from each parent, every limitation cites a source, and declared levels and provenance are present. A no-fusion report is checked for its required structure: distinct candidates, cited property ids, and a documented rejection reason for each. Every output of either type is recorded as an attempt. An output that fails any check is recorded as a failed attempt and is not reviewed further.
- **Quality is decided by blind review.** Independent reviewers judge the fusion card itself. Your causal explanation and declarations are removed before they see it, so the card's mechanism has to stand on its own.
- **Your declarations are claims.** Declared levels and provenance are compared with the reviewers' independent judgments. They are not evidence of achievement.
- **No-fusion reports are checked too.** Independent reviewers search the same two profiles for a defensible fusion you may have missed, and the reasons you give for rejecting each candidate are verified.
- **Nothing here is approval.** Passing mechanical checks or review makes a proposal eligible; a human operator decides whether any fusion is approved.

**Honesty requirements**

- Prefer a no-fusion report to a fabricated connection.
- Declare the level you can support, not the highest level you can argue for.
- Never state that a candidate fails a test it actually passes, or that a property says something it does not.
- Disclose what you are unsure of.
