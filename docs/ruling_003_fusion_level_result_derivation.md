## 2. Terms — SCOPE

**Substantive component.** A component presented to the resolution pipeline as an input concept,
the outcome of whose resolution this Ruling's §4.1 takes into account. This term does not use or
decide Ruling 001 §7.4's deferred participation-in-grounding-eligibility question.

**Identification failure.** The terminal condition in which a substantive component's concept
"could not be identified at all," corresponding to the `BLOCKED` row of Contract 001 §5.1
(`contract_001_domain_resolution.md:202`). It does not include a component that was identified but
later halted at domain, family, or grounding resolution.

**Successfully resolved.** A substantive component is successfully resolved when it completes
domain, family, and grounding resolution, where the grounding outcome is either a grounded
candidate or `grounding not required`. ("Successfully grounded" is not used as a substitute for
this definition.)

**Domain/family-stage halt.** A substantive component whose terminal state is
`UNRESOLVED_DOMAIN`, `AMBIGUOUS_DOMAIN`, or `UNRESOLVED_FAMILY`.

`ADMITTED`, `SURFACED`, `CAUTIONARY`, and `BLOCKED` are used as Ruling 001 §4 defines them and as
Contract 001 §5.1 maps them, unaltered; §10 states this Ruling's construction of Ruling 001 §4's
`SURFACED` row. `GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE` is used as Contract 001 §5 lists it among the
grounding-resolution states, sourced there to Ruling 002 §5.2 and §5.3
(`contract_001_domain_resolution.md:195`), unaltered.

The terminal-condition vocabulary aggregated by §4.1 is limited to the Contract 001 §5.1 pipeline
conditions stated in this Ruling's §3.1; this Ruling does not determine aggregation behavior for any other
condition that may independently produce a `BLOCKED` result under Ruling 001 §4.

## 3. What existing text already governs — CARRIED

## 4. Result policy — NEW B3 POLICY

**4.1 Ordered precedence.** Each rule is tested only after every prior rule fails:

1. Any substantive-component identification failure has absolute precedence → `BLOCKED`.
2. Otherwise, if any substantive component reaches `GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE` → `SURFACED`,
   regardless of whether the fusion also contains successfully resolved components,
   domain/family-stage halts, or both.
3. Otherwise, if at least one substantive component has a domain/family-stage halt and at least
   one substantive component is successfully resolved → `SURFACED`.
4. Otherwise, if substantive components have domain/family-stage halts and no substantive
   component is successfully resolved → `CAUTIONARY`.
5. Otherwise, every substantive component is successfully resolved → `ADMITTED`.

**4.2** This is an ordered-precedence rule, not a numeric success-count threshold.

**4.3 Which parts are new.** The following are new B3 policy:

- the identification-failure veto across mixed-component outcomes;
- success + domain/family-stage halt → `SURFACED`;
- all-`GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE`, zero-success → `SURFACED`. `ruling_001_canonicalization_policy.md:202`
  (Fusion B) is not used as proof that this was already governed: Fusion B is tied to Regression
  Case 001 (`ruling_001_canonicalization_policy.md:207`, §6), a two-component fusion (Hashirama
  Senju × William Vangeance), and William Vangeance's corrected-path resolution outcome is
  unstated anywhere in the locked text. Fusion B is neutral on the zero-success question, not
  supportive of it;
- `GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE` + domain/family-stage halt, zero-success → `SURFACED`;
- success + `GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE` + domain/family-stage halt → `SURFACED`
  (the three-way case; §5).

The remaining branches (uniform success via either successful-resolution path; success +
`GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE` only; domain/family-only zero-success, uniform or multi-cause) are not
classified by this Ruling as new B3 policy. This Ruling makes no further provenance determination
about those branches.

**4.4 Zero success does not by itself imply `CAUTIONARY`.** No rule of this Ruling assigns
`CAUTIONARY` merely because no component is successfully resolved; rule 4 applies only after
rules 1–3 have failed to match. `SURFACED` does not require at least one successfully resolved
component.

## 5. The three-way case — NEW B3 POLICY

`success + GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE + domain/family-stage halt → SURFACED` is new B3 policy at the
fusion-result level, not already governed directly, because existing text (Ruling 001 §4, Ruling
002 §8.1, Contract 001 §5.1) assigns the constituent conditions to different rows individually and
does not resolve their simultaneous cross-row aggregation (§3.2). The result is produced by rule 2
of §4.1. Preservation and reporting of the newly-included domain/family-halted component in this
branch is likewise new B3 territory, and carries the consequence stated in §8.

## 6. CAUTIONARY diagnostics

### 6.1 What is governed — CARRIED

Governing text requires a `CAUTIONARY` result's reason to travel with the result and prohibits its
silent loss (§3.3). Nothing in this Ruling extends that requirement.

### 6.2 What the governing passage does not address — OPEN — NOT DECIDED

The passage at §3.3 does not address a fusion in which multiple `CAUTIONARY`-triggering causes
coexist. Contract 003 I5 additionally provides that every terminal state carries its reason to
the output (`contract_003_gap5_surfaced_payload_preservation.md:193-194`). I5 does not state
whether "terminal state" is per-component, fusion-level, or both. Under a per-component reading it
supports all applicable component reasons traveling, but does not expressly govern coexistence of
multiple `CAUTIONARY` causes or require per-component diagnostic attribution. Accordingly, I5 does
not resolve either question below. Two questions follow, and both are open:

- **(a) All-causes-travel.** Whether all applicable causes must travel when multiple
  `CAUTIONARY`-triggering causes coexist in one fusion. The stated rationale of Contract 001 §5.1
  strongly supports it, and Contract 003 I5 provides additional support under the per-component
  reading stated above, but neither text expressly establishes an all-causes-travel requirement
  for coexisting `CAUTIONARY` causes. It is not presently governed. This Ruling adopts no
  all-causes-travel rule.
- **(b) Per-component attribution.** Whether a diagnostic must identify which substantive
  component produced which `CAUTIONARY` cause. Not governed.

### 6.3 Limits on both questions — OPEN — NOT DECIDED

Neither question changes which fusion-level result §4 assigns; both are outside this Ruling's
necessary classification scope. No answer is adopted for either. Both are owned by this Ruling and
remain open; ownership adopts no answer to either.
No gap is opened for either. No implementation requirement is inferred for either, and no
partial version of either proposition is adopted: the support that Contract 001 §5.1's rationale lends to (a)
is recorded as support and is not promoted to a requirement.

## 8. The Contract 003 I9(c)/(f) payload-coverage consequence — CONTINGENT CONSEQUENCE

The policy in §4 permits a `SURFACED` fusion to contain substantive components halted at
`UNRESOLVED_DOMAIN`, `AMBIGUOUS_DOMAIN`, `UNRESOLVED_FAMILY`, and/or `GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE`, with
or without another successfully resolved substantive component.

**This consequence is contingent on this Ruling becoming operative. Until it does, the premise on
which Disposition 003 §2.2 narrowed "unresolved/rejected" to `GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE` remains
unchanged, and this Ruling identifies no present defect in I9 or in GAP-5's recorded closure.**

Disposition 003 §2.2 excluded `UNRESOLVED_DOMAIN`, `AMBIGUOUS_DOMAIN`, and `UNRESOLVED_FAMILY`
from its `SURFACED` payload determination because those states map to `CAUTIONARY`, not
`SURFACED` (`disposition_003_gap5_surfaced_payload_policy.md:83-91`). That section expressly
declined to decide "how a fusion's overall result is classified when its components' individual
terminal states differ across stages," referring that question to its §1 scope exclusion of Ruling
002 §10.3. GAP-5 is CLOSED; its recorded closure condition is used here as comparative evidence
only. That closure condition requires an eventual invariant to preserve and
report the components that resolved successfully, the components that did not resolve or were
rejected, and the governed reason for those that did not resolve, without permitting the accepted
portion of a fusion to be discarded merely because another component terminates unsuccessfully
(`open_contract_gaps.md:974-983`).

**If this Ruling becomes operative as drafted, it removes that premise for mixed-stage fusions:
domain/family-halted components can then occur inside a fusion classified `SURFACED`. For those
newly-`SURFACED` cases, I9(c)'s `GROUNDING_UNAVAILABLE`-only preservation scope — and I9(f)'s
anti-discard protection, which is keyed to components preserved under (c) — would no longer cover
the full recorded closure-condition text requiring preservation/reporting of components that "did
not resolve or were rejected."**

**The resulting governance consequence — the residual Contract 003 payload-coverage question —
requires a later, separately authorized determination. This Ruling does not classify or number
it. This Ruling neither selects the remedy nor determines whether that consequence is recorded
through supersession, a new invariant, a correction instrument, a gap act, or another governed
route. It does not modify I9, reopen GAP-5, characterize I9 as defective today, or characterize
GAP-5's closure as defective today.**
